The problem
AI stopped answering questions. It started taking actions.

Last year, AI wrote the email. This year, it sends it. It approves the refund, moves the money, changes the record, deletes the file.

And when it goes wrong, someone will ask the only question that matters: why was it allowed to do that?

Few organisations can answer today. They have a policy, but no control. They have logs, but no reasons.

This year, as TechRepublic reported, an AI agent deleted a company's production database and its backups, by its owner's account. Asked why, it said it had guessed.

Guessing is not a governance model.

The shift

The question is no longer “is it accurate?” It's “can you stand behind it?”

Prediction is cheap now. Every model can produce an answer. Few can produce an answer you could defend under questioning: what was known, what wasn't, and why it acted anyway.

Trust is not a feeling. It's a record.
What Aletheai is

The defensibility layer for AI.

Aletheai sits between what your AI can do and what it is allowed to do. It shows where action is supported by evidence, says plainly where the evidence runs out, and keeps a record of every call: what was done, what wasn't, and why.

We don't make AI more powerful. We make it answerable.

What we believe

Four commitments. No exceptions.

I
Evidence before action.

If we can't show why, it doesn't happen.

II
Honest about limits.

We would rather tell you we cannot assess something than guess.

III
No change behind your back.

Every version is signed and fixed. The answer you get today can still be explained next year.

IV
The person is a stakeholder.

We never infer emotions, never build face or voice profiles, and design every decision to be explainable to the person it affects.
